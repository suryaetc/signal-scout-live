Superstocks — Tier 2
What is this page?

This is the Superstocks page — Tier 2 of Signal Scout, a systematic momentum investing system. It lists stocks that have triggered our rules-based entry signal after passing a multi-stage screen. These are not recommendations — they are signals generated entirely by rules, on the record.

How to read the numbers

All figures come from our backtest of every Superstock entry, with exits on the system's own rules and no hindsight in the entry data. Headline figures cover Jan 2014 – Aug 2026, the period closest to today's market; the full 20-year test (from Nov 2006) is used as a cross-check. Each entry is one trade:

Win rateShare of trades that closed in profit.
MedianThe middle trade's gain, entry to exit.
CAGR heldThe yearly compounding rate of the money while it was in these trades. A per-trade measure — not a portfolio return.

Across all entries since 2014: 59% win rate, +8% median, 21% CAGR held. Past backtest results do not guarantee future returns.

Entry Grade — A / B / C / D

Every entry is graded once, on the day it triggers, using only information available that day. The grade combines several measures of trend quality, relative strength and how stretched the entry is into one letter. It is frozen at entry and never changes.

ABest entries — 64% win rate, +10% median, 30% CAGR held. Shortest typical hold (about 6 months).
BGood entries — 62% / +10% / 26%.
CAverage entries — 60% / +9% / 22%.
DWeakest entries — 52% / +3% / 12%, and the most trades losing 20% or more.

Since 2014, A/B entries beat C/D entries in 11 of 13 entry years, and the gap is widest when the broad market is in an uptrend (A: 74% win, 50% CAGR held). The same order held in 2006–2013. A grade describes the entry; it is not a buy or sell instruction.

Strength — market leadership at entry

How many of the stock, its industry and its sector were outperforming the broad market (NSE 500) when the entry triggered. STRONG++ = all three, STRONG = two.

Since 2014, STRONG++ entries won 69% with 41% CAGR held, against 55% / 14% when none of the three was leading. But year by year it was not steady: inside grade A/B it added in only 6 of 13 years, with much of the average coming from 2020. So Strength is used in only one tier, Prime+, with that caution attached.

IPO Tab — Ranking Score

Newly listed stocks don't yet have enough monthly history for a grade or Strength, so the IPO tab uses a dedicated Ranking Score instead — relative strength, how close the entry was to the breakout level (extension), and whether the stock's industry is leading or lagging the market, combined into one number.

ExtensionHow far above the weekly breakout the entry was. An entry close to the breakout is stronger than chasing a name already 8%+ extended.
RS @ EntryRelative strength vs the market, frozen at the entry date so it doesn't drift with hindsight.
IndustryLeading / Weak / Avoid — is the stock's industry itself in an uptrend right now.
Ranking ScoreThe combined score. Higher is a higher-conviction IPO signal; ✕ EXIT marks a failed breakout — a weekly close back below the fractal.
Exit Signals

A position is held until one of three rule-based exits triggers. Until then, the stop status reads SAFE or WATCH.

SAFEStop intact — within normal range.
WATCHApproaching an exit zone — monitor.
EXHAUSTIONStrength exhaustion exit — momentum has faded and the system steps out of a winner.
DYNAMIC SLDynamic stop — a trailing stop that ratchets up as the trade works, protecting open profit.
FIXED SLFixed (initial) stop — the stop-loss set at entry, capping downside if the trade fails early.
